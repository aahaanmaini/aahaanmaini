# Hi, I'm Aahaan!  

I'm a machine learning and research engineer currently studying Software Engineering at the University of Waterloo. At heart, I'm a builder who learns by shipping, breaking, and iterating, and the work I'm proudest of usually starts as a problem I can't stop thinking about, the kind that sits in the back of your mind, constantly turning over until the solution clicks.

Most of those problems sit downstream of the bitter lesson, the idea that the methods that win in the long run are the ones that scale with increased compute. A few are top of mind for me right now:

- How do you build evals for unverifiable domains and turn them into a signal you can actually steer the model with?
- What's the right minimal scaffold for an agent, lean enough that the model owns the reasoning instead of getting buried under prompts and rules?
- Where does test-time compute get spent best?
- How do you design products around models that get noticeably better every few months, instead of around the one in front of you today?

I'm also interested in post-training as the place where most of a model's usefulness gets shaped.
